# 7. Chủ đề bất ngờ (Unexpected Topics)

Đây là những chủ đề **không có trong Background Survey** nhưng vẫn hay xuất hiện. Không cần học sâu, chỉ cần **1 bài khung cho mỗi chủ đề** để không bị "đứng hình".

> 💡 **Mẹo:** Khi gặp chủ đề lạ, hãy **kéo về trải nghiệm của bạn**. Ví dụ hỏi về ngân hàng → kể lần bạn đi mở tài khoản. Hỏi về tái chế → kể cách khu nhà bạn phân loại rác.

---

## 7.1 Công nghệ (Technology)

> **Q:** *"Tell me about a piece of technology you use often. How has it changed your life?"*

> The piece of technology I use the most is definitely my smartphone. I honestly can't imagine a single day without it.
>
> I use it for almost everything. In the morning, it wakes me up with an alarm. On my way to work, I read the news or listen to podcasts. At work, I use it to check emails and message my coworkers. And in the evening, I use it to order food, pay bills, or watch videos.
>
> It has changed my life in many ways. The biggest change is convenience. For example, a few years ago I had to go to the bank to transfer money, but now I can do it in a few seconds with a banking app. I also use apps to learn English, which is really helpful.
>
> But there are also some downsides. Sometimes I spend way too much time on social media, and I end up going to bed really late. So recently, I've started turning on a screen-time limit to control it.
>
> Overall, my smartphone makes my life much easier, but I'm trying to use it more wisely.

## 7.2 Tái chế (Recycling)

> **Q:** *"Describe how people recycle in your country. What do you do to recycle?"*

> Recycling in Vietnam is improving, but I think there's still a long way to go.
>
> In big cities like Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, there are now some programs that ask people to separate their waste into different types — like organic waste, recyclable waste, and other trash. In my apartment building, there are separate bins for plastic, paper, and general waste near the elevator.
>
> Also, in Vietnam we have an interesting system. There are people who go around collecting things like cardboard, plastic bottles, and old cans. They buy them from households for a small amount of money and then sell them to recycling centers. So a lot of recycling actually happens this way.
>
> Personally, I try to do my part. I keep plastic bottles and cardboard boxes separately and give them to the collector in my neighborhood. I also bring my own bag when I go shopping and try to use a reusable water bottle instead of buying bottled water.
>
> I think if more people did small things like this, it would make a big difference.

## 7.3 Thời tiết (Weather)

> **Q:** *"Describe the weather in your country. How does it change from season to season?"*

> The weather in Vietnam is quite different depending on the region.
>
> In the north, where I live, we have four seasons. Spring is cool and a little bit drizzly. Summer is extremely hot and humid — sometimes it goes up to almost forty degrees, and there are a lot of thunderstorms. Fall is my favorite season. It's cool, dry, and the sky is clear, so it's perfect for going outside. And winter is cold and damp. It doesn't snow, except on a few high mountains, but it can feel really chilly because of the humidity.
>
> In the south, on the other hand, there are only two seasons: the dry season and the rainy season. It's hot pretty much all year round. During the rainy season, it usually rains heavily for an hour or so in the afternoon, and then the sun comes out again.
>
> Recently, I feel like the weather has become more extreme. Summers are hotter than before, and we have more floods and storms. I think it's probably because of climate change.

## 7.4 Ngày lễ (Holidays)

> **Q:** *"Tell me about a holiday that is important in your country. What do people do on that day?"*

> The most important holiday in Vietnam is Tet, or the Lunar New Year. It usually falls in late January or February.
>
> Before Tet, people are really busy. They clean and decorate their houses, buy new clothes, and prepare traditional food. One of the most famous dishes is banh chung, a square sticky rice cake filled with pork and mung beans. People also buy peach blossoms in the north or yellow apricot blossoms in the south to decorate their homes.
>
> On New Year's Eve, families gather together for a big dinner, and at midnight many people watch fireworks. During the first few days of the new year, we visit our relatives, friends, and teachers to wish them good luck and good health. Children receive lucky money in red envelopes, which is their favorite part, of course.
>
> For me, Tet is the most special time of the year because it's the only time when my whole family, including relatives who live far away, can get together. No matter how busy I am, I always go back to my hometown for Tet.

## 7.5 Nhà hàng & ăn ngoài (Restaurants)

> **Q:** *"Tell me about a restaurant you like to go to. Describe it."*

> There's a small Japanese restaurant near my office that I really like. I go there at least once a week, usually for lunch.
>
> It's not a fancy place. It only has about ten tables, and the interior is simple, with wooden tables and warm yellow lights. There's also a counter where you can sit and watch the chef make sushi, which is really fun.
>
> My go-to dish there is the salmon rice bowl. The salmon is always fresh, and the portion is big for the price. They also have really good miso soup and green tea, which come free with every meal.
>
> What I like most is the service. The staff are always friendly, and they remember my usual order, so I feel like a regular there. Also, it's never too loud, so it's a nice place to take a break from work.
>
> The only problem is that it gets really crowded at lunchtime, so I sometimes have to wait ten or fifteen minutes for a table. But it's definitely worth the wait.
