# 6. Du lịch trong nước (Domestic Travel)

---

## Câu 1 – Nơi thích đi du lịch

> **Q:** *"You indicated that you like to travel domestically. Where do you like to go and why?"*

> I love traveling around Vietnam, and if I had to choose one place, it would definitely be Da Lat.
>
> Da Lat is a city in the Central Highlands, and it's famous for its cool weather all year round. Since I live in Ho Chi Minh City, where it's hot almost every day, going to Da Lat feels like a breath of fresh air — literally.
>
> The scenery there is beautiful. There are pine forests, waterfalls, flower gardens, and lots of hills covered in fog in the early morning. The city also has a lot of charming cafés with amazing views, and I could honestly spend the whole day just sitting in one of them with a cup of hot coffee.
>
> Another reason I like Da Lat is the food. At night, the night market is full of street food, like grilled rice paper, which people call "Vietnamese pizza," and hot soy milk. It's perfect for the chilly evenings.
>
> It's only about an hour by plane or seven hours by bus from my city, so it's easy to go there for a long weekend.

## Câu 2 – Chuẩn bị trước chuyến đi

> **Q:** *"What do you usually do to prepare for a trip?"*

> I'm a pretty organized person, so I always plan my trips carefully.
>
> First, about a month before the trip, I decide where to go and check the weather forecast for that time. Then I book the flight or bus tickets and a place to stay. I usually look for homestays instead of hotels, because they're cheaper and the hosts often give great local tips.
>
> Next, I make a rough plan for each day. I look up reviews and travel blogs to find the must-see places and good local restaurants. But I don't plan every hour — I like to leave some free time to just wander around.
>
> A couple of days before I leave, I start packing. I always make a checklist so I don't forget important things like my ID card, phone charger, power bank, and medicine. I try to pack light, so I usually bring just one backpack.
>
> Finally, the night before, I double-check my tickets and booking confirmations. Planning ahead like this helps me avoid stress and enjoy the trip more.

## Câu 3 – Kinh nghiệm du lịch đáng nhớ / có sự cố

> **Q:** *"Tell me about a memorable trip. What happened, and why was it so memorable?"*

> One trip that I'll never forget was my trip to Ha Giang with three friends about two years ago.
>
> Ha Giang is a mountainous province in the far north of Vietnam, and it's famous for its winding roads and stunning views. We decided to rent motorbikes and do the Ha Giang loop, which takes about four days.
>
> On the second day, we were riding up a mountain pass called Ma Pi Leng. The view was absolutely breathtaking — deep valleys, a green river far below, and mountains everywhere. We kept stopping to take pictures.
>
> But in the afternoon, something went wrong. My friend's motorbike got a flat tire in the middle of nowhere, and there was no phone signal. We waited for almost an hour, and it was getting dark. Finally, a local man on a motorbike passed by. He didn't speak much Vietnamese, but he understood our problem and led us to a small repair shop in the next village.
>
> We were so grateful that we invited him to dinner, and he ended up inviting us to his home to meet his family.
>
> That trip taught me that sometimes the unexpected problems turn into the best memories. And it showed me how kind people can be.

## Câu 4 – Du lịch xưa và nay

> **Q:** *"How has travel in your country changed compared to the past?"*

> Travel in Vietnam has changed a lot over the past ten or fifteen years.
>
> In the past, traveling wasn't that common. Most families only traveled once a year, usually during summer vacation or Tet, and they often went by bus or train because flights were expensive. Also, planning was harder — you had to call hotels directly or book through a travel agency.
>
> Nowadays, traveling is much easier and more affordable. There are many low-cost airlines, so you can fly from the south to the north for a reasonable price. And with apps and websites, you can book everything — flights, hotels, even tours — in just a few minutes on your phone.
>
> Another big change is how young people travel. They prefer backpacking and exploring by themselves rather than going on package tours. And of course, social media plays a big role. A lot of people choose destinations because they saw beautiful photos on Instagram or TikTok.
>
> However, one negative side is that popular places have become really crowded, and some of them are not as clean and natural as before.
